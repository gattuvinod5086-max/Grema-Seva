The Challenge

Opportunities for coordinated digital improvement

  • Citizens may not know where to report a village problem
  • Complaints can move through multiple administrative levels
  • Citizens need visibility into complaint progress
  • Officials need a consolidated view of pending and overdue issues
  • Welfare and local information are spread across different channels

Today: Multiple channels

Phone · Office visit · Paper · Messages · Informal communication

→ Need: One coordinated digital layer

From Problem to Resolution

Citizen→Report→Classify→Route→ Assign→SLA→Resolve→Confirm→Analytics
  • Report: Text / Voice / Photo
  • Classify: Category + Priority
  • Route: Village / Department
  • Assign: Responsible Official
  • SLA: Time-bound action
  • Resolve: Action + Evidence
  • Confirm: Citizen feedback
  • Analytics: Measure outcomes

Accountability Through SLA & Escalation

Proposed configurable workflow

Citizen→Ward Member→Sarpanch→Mandal→District
  • High priority example: 24-hour acknowledgement SLA (configurable)
  • If not acknowledged → escalate · If unresolved → escalate further
  • ● Within SLA   ● Approaching   ● SLA Breached
  • Not official Telangana policy — proposed workflow for pilot evaluation

Every Action is Traceable

Auditable grievance lifecycle

Submitted→Acknowledged→Assigned→ In Progress→Evidence→Resolved→Citizen Confirm
  • Each step records: Actor · Role · Timestamp · Action
  • Previous state → New state captured
  • Resolution is not just a status change — it is an auditable workflow

Resolution Should Be Verified by the Citizen

  • Official marks: "Resolved"
  • Citizen asked: "Was your problem actually resolved?"
  • YES → Closed with citizen confirmation
  • NO → Reopen · Escalate · Continue resolution
  • Separates government-side closure from citizen-side satisfaction
